Method in brief

Resident sports attention A flows through teams, into directed rivalries R = S⊙T, and lands on the state identity each rival embodies (E): H = A(S⊙T)E; pair score M = √(HabHba). Attention A = W × P: W is each state's sport mix (Google Trends topic terms, blended with national survey priors), P splits a sport among teams (anchored Google Trends regional shares, blended with population-weighted county-plurality maps). Trends data are integer percent shares among 5 terms per state; two anchor terms per batch put batches on one scale; zeros are treated as below detection, never as true zero.

Limitations and what is not done

  • No winner is declared. Illinois-Wisconsin is the favorite under the model's own assumptions, but four gates fail (robustness under alternative tier and sport-weight assumptions, dominance of its biggest matchup untestable on the corrected measure, and a contradicting second proxy). The full register is in docs/LIMITATIONS.md.
  • The rivalry layer (S, T) is my prior (tiers .40/.20/.08 and .85/.65/.45), tested against measured antipathy searches and Wikipedia readership; the flat-tier and flat-sport-weight variants show how much the answer depends on those judgments.
  • Entity-to-state identity (E) is rule-based (home state 95% with documented exceptions) with only an exploratory measurement.
  • Most college team-by-state cells are below detection (censored; carried as bounded floors, never zero), and the small regional sports sit at the detection floor, so their sport-level weights are upper bounds.
  • County-plurality maps measure population living in plurality counties, not fan share; the NHL map predates the Utah relocation.
  • Google Trends via an unofficial endpoint: relative, rounded, rate-limited; raw payloads are not published, so only stages downstream of the local cache are re-runnable.
  • The Monte Carlo samples measurement and parameter noise but not structural alternatives (tier ladder, sport weights), which change the favorite; "first in about two thirds of runs" is conditional on the model's structure.

Reproduce

See README.md. python scripts/finalize.py rebuilds everything from the local Google Trends cache; python scripts/finalize.py --from-derived re-runs the model, charts and verdict from the committed derived tables (it reuses the committed uncertainty outputs). Raw Trends payloads are not published.
