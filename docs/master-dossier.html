01 · Scope

The exact question

Among the 1,225 unordered pairs of the 50 U.S. states, which pair has the greatest current, reciprocal, population-penetrating sports antagonism across meaningful professional and collegiate team sports?

Current

The primary empirical window is roughly the five most recent seasons as of September 15, 2026. History may explain persistence; it does not receive automatic points.

Reciprocal

A state pair must matter in both directions. One state obsessing over another while receiving indifference is penalized by the geometric-mean pair score.

Population-penetrating

The model follows residents’ sports attention. A stadium sitting inside a border does not, by itself, make a franchise the identity of that state.

What the study explicitly refuses to do

  • No preselected finalist list. Every state pair remains eligible until the same rules eliminate it.
  • No points merely for sharing a border, geographic closeness, politics, cultural stereotypes, championships, fights, or memorable individual games.
  • No equation equating team location with fan residence or with the state identity embodied by that team.
  • No deletion of in-state, D.C., Canadian, or other non-winning rivals when they consume finite rivalry attention.
  • No treating missing observations as observed zeroes.
  • No manual boost because a rivalry “feels important” or because a favored candidate otherwise looks too weak.
  • No declaring the winner until the bottom-up model, structural stress tests, independent top-down validation, and uncertainty analysis are complete.
02 · Engine

The mathematical engine

The model traces a resident’s finite sports attention through a team or program, into a directed rivalry, and finally toward the state identity embodied by the opponent.

AAttentionHow State s distributes sports attention across entities i.
R = S ⊙ TRivalryHow entity i directs salience and emotional heat toward entity j.
EEmbodimentHow strongly entity j represents geographic identity b.
H = AREHostilityDirected hostility flowing from residents of state a toward state b.
A ∈ ℝ50×I,   Σᵢ Asi = 1
Sij = directed rivalry salience;   Tij ∈ [0,1] = directed emotional temperature
R = S ⊙ T,   Rij = 0 whenever Kij = 0
E ∈ ℝI×B,   Σb Eib ≤ 1
H = A R E = A(S ⊙ T)E
Mab = √(HabHba)

Why the score is geometric

The sum would reward one-sided obsession. With the geometric mean, directed scores of .50 and .10 produce .224, while balanced scores of .30 and .30 produce .300. Harmonic mean and minimum remain robustness alternatives, not the primary rule.

Why the graph is directed

A→B and B→A are separate measurements. Fans routinely allocate dramatically different rivalry attention in opposite directions. Symmetric labels never justify symmetric weights.

04 · Complete

Phase 1 — Protocol v1.0

Phase 1 froze the question and scoring architecture before any contender could influence the rules.

Unit of analysis

State-resident sports attention. A “state” acts through the aggregate fan culture of its residents, not through legal ownership, venue address, or a team’s official mailing address.

Entity definition

A professional franchise/team, or a college institution × sport × sex/category program. Ohio State football and Ohio State women’s hockey are distinct entities.

Eligible competition

Geographically or institutionally anchored team competition with organized fandom, genuine opponent-based rivalry, and enough evidence to estimate its contribution to a state’s sports culture.

Currentness

Main empirical evidence centers on the most recent five seasons nearest September 15, 2026. Persistent history informs classification and diagnostics, not arbitrary score additions.

Scope inclusions and exclusions

CategoryProtocol treatmentReason
Major professional team sportsIncludedAnchored entities, large fandom, rival structures, measurable geography.
Division I college varsity sportsCandidate by defaultInstitutional identity can dominate state culture even outside the largest sports.
Lower-division varsity / NAIAConditionalIncluded when state attention or rivalry evidence clears common thresholds.
Minor / alternative professional leaguesTeam-by-team conditionalLeague membership alone does not prove meaningful statewide relevance.
High-school sportsExcludedToo diffuse, localized, and inconsistently measurable for the national state-pair model.
Club, intramural, ordinary recreational sportsExcludedOutside the anchored, organized fan-attention unit.
International national teamsExcludedDo not map cleanly into state-versus-state identities.
Pure individual-athlete sportsGenerally excludedInsufficient stable opponent/state anchoring; exceptional team formats require evidence.
MotorsportsGenerally excludedDriver/manufacturer fandom rarely maps into persistent interstate opponent identities.

Protocol safeguards

  • Rules are frozen before results; revisions must be global and documented.
  • Every source has date, scope, provenance, quality, and missingness metadata.
  • Observed zero, structural zero, below-detection, missing, and not-applicable are different states.
  • Asymmetric rivalry and asymmetric state identity are permitted.
  • Shared fandom can reduce interstate hostility by consuming or redirecting attention.
  • Every final score must decompose into auditable entity-to-entity channels.
  • Unexpected winners are accepted if they survive the same evidence and robustness rules.

College and conditional professional universe

College

All Division I varsity programs are automatic candidates. Football distinguishes FBS and FCS. Lower divisions and NAIA enter conditionally. Every record is institution × sport × sex/category; school brand may be a weak prior but never substitutes for sport-specific attention.

Other professional

League-agnostic screening permits UFL, USL, professional volleyball, and other anchored leagues or teams when common attention and evidence rules are met. Formal existence alone is not relevance.

Active-universe logic

Active entity if maxₛ A(0)si ≥ 0.0025
OR entity is required to reach ≈ 98% modeled attention in a state
OR a retained rivalry opponent reaches Sij ≥ 0.05
Unexplained attention remains visible as OTHER.

The 0.25% preliminary threshold is intentionally high-recall, not a final weight. The 98% rule catches concentrated omissions. Opponent activation prevents a seemingly small entity from disappearing when it absorbs meaningful rivalry attention from a large entity.

Compatibility mask K

Kij=1 means two entities could plausibly sustain an opponent-based rivalry under the sport’s actual competition structure. It does not mean they do. Same-sport regular season, conference, postseason, interleague, cup, historic scheduling, or other genuine competitive paths can qualify. Different sports do not form team-to-team rivalry edges merely because their states overlap. Conference or division membership is opportunity, never automatic rivalry.

Phase 3B — National rivalry discovery

This phase decided which directed rivalry edges could plausibly be nonzero. It deliberately stopped before assigning current emotional weights.

For survey-covered competitions: Sdiscoveryij ≥ 0.05
Equivalent to ≥ 5 of 100 rivalry points in Know Rivalry’s fan-allocation framework.

Evidence classes

ClassMeaningTypical evidenceModel treatment
R1Direct measuredFans quantitatively allocate rivalry attention.Strongest discovery evidence; still subject to recency/currentness review.
R2Explicit contemporary rivalryLeague/team rivalry labeling, annual trophy, rivalry week.Edge retained; Phase 5 measures direction and heat.
R3Strong inferred rivalryMultiple independent current sources and repeated scheduling traditions.Retained with greater measurement burden.
R4Emerging / provisionalNew expansion geography, marketing, or recent postseason conflict.Conservative prior; no mature hatred assumed.

Edge lifecycle

Every edge is labeled current-active, current-intermittent, dormant, or historical. Only the first two generate substantial primary-model R. Dormant/historical relationships move to temporal diagnostics.

Finite rivalry attentionIf Michigan fans devote major attention to Michigan State, that mass is unavailable to flow toward Ohio. Deleting same-state edges would inflate interstate hostility.
Discovery is not scoringA named or measured rivalry survives into Phase 5; it does not receive an emotional-intensity number here.

Phase 4A — State sports-attention model

The attention matrix is specified as a hierarchy: first the share of each state’s sports attention going to each sport, then the division of that sport’s attention among teams or programs.

Aₛᵢ = Wₛₖ × Pₛᵢ|ₖ
Σₖ Wₛₖ = 1    and    Σi∈k Pₛᵢ|ₖ = 1
InterpretationIf 12% of North Dakota’s sports attention goes to hockey and 80% of its NHL attention points to one NHL team, that entity receives 9.6% of the state’s total modeled sports attention—not 80%.
4A.1–4A.3 · Decomposition and primary signal

Wsk measures state sport-level attention. Psi|k allocates that attention among entities within the sport. Google Trends is the principal accessible state-specific signal because it normalizes by total searches within geography and time, limiting raw population distortion.

4A.4 · Use Google Topics, not ambiguous strings

Use entity/topic definitions such as “Minnesota Vikings — American football team,” not bare terms like Vikings, Wild, Heat, Jazz, Giants or Rangers. Store entity_id, topic/query identifier, query type, and ambiguity flag.

4A.5–4A.6 · Five-year window, recency decay, persistent and current features

Primary Trends window: approximately September 2021 through September 2026. Apply exponential recency weighting with a 24-month half-life:

w(t) = 2−t/24

Retain Gᴾsi, the five-year recency-weighted persistent baseline, and Gᶜsi, the most recent 12-month current signal. Persistent interest drives baseline A; current interest supplies a controlled adjustment.

4A.7 · Anchored query batching

Because Google Trends comparisons have item limits and batch scales are not automatically commensurable, every batch uses a common sport anchor. The anchor rescales separate comparison groups onto a common coordinate system.

4A.8–4A.10 · County fandom and geographic penetration

Vivid Seats county/state ticket-origin maps provide a signal independent of searches for NFL, MLB, NBA, MLS, college football, WNBA and an older NHL map. County plurality must be population-weighted using Census Vintage 2025:

Cₛᵢ = [Σc∈s Popc · I(i = plurality team in c)] / Popₛ

This is population living in counties where i is the plurality—not true fan share. Preserve county rows so Phase 8 can distinguish statewide, metro, regional, and fragmented fandom.

4A.11 · National surveys are priors, not state answers

Captured 2025 national viewing priors include approximately NFL 43%, MLB 29%, NBA 24%, NHL 17%; among football fans, 55% watched college football. Captured attendance priors were roughly NFL 20%, MLB 18%, NBA 14%, NHL 9%. These inform national baselines, never Minnesota- or Alabama-specific allocation.

4A.12–4A.14 · Sport-level ensemble W and missingness
log W̃ₛₖ = βGlog Gₛₖ + βNlog Nₖ + βVlog Vₛₖ
Wₛₖ = W̃ₛₖ / Σₗ W̃ₛₗ

Use a weighted geometric ensemble. When a source is missing, renormalize source weights across present evidence; do not insert zero. Exact β values are deliberately unfrozen until overlapping sources, held-out validation, and sensitivity can estimate plausible reliability.

4A.15–4A.16 · Attendance is calibration, not a raw ingredient

Attendance is structurally biased by schedule length, venue capacity, prices, and home-date count. It acts mainly as calibration and anomaly detection. Where useful, transform it:

Lₛᵢ = average home attendance / state population
L*ₛᵢ = annual unique or estimated attendance exposure / state population
Uᵢ = attendance / capacity

Never compare raw 81-game MLB totals directly with seven football dates.

4A.17–4A.18 · Within-sport allocation P and smoothing
log P̃ₛᵢ|ₖ = γGlog Gₛᵢ + γClog Cₛᵢ + γDlog Dₛᵢ + γTlog TKTₛᵢ
Pₛᵢ|ₖ = P̃ₛᵢ / Σj∈kP̃ₛⱼ

Signals are Google interest, population-weighted county plurality, direct survey/favorite-team evidence, and richer ticket-origin data where obtainable. Replace numeric zero with a tiny floor x′=max(x, ε) to avoid log-zero and false claims of absolute nonexistence.

4A.19–4A.20 · Out-of-state fandom and multi-team states

Kansas may assign enormous NFL attention to the Chiefs; that is legitimate A. E later decides what state identity the Chiefs embody. Texas can divide NFL attention among Cowboys, Texans and national spillover; Pennsylvania among Eagles and Steelers; Ohio among Browns, Bengals and nearby spillover; California and New York remain multi-market.

4A.21–4A.22 · College and regional sports

Every program × sport is measured separately. School brand supplies only a weak prior when sport-specific evidence is thin. Passing Phase 3A forces proper investigation for Iowa wrestling, Nebraska volleyball, North Dakota hockey, LSU baseball, Oklahoma softball and similar entities; it does not grant them automatic A mass.

4A.23–4A.24 · Residual OTHER and 98% audit

Each sport/state retains an OTHER entity. At state level:

Oₛ = 1 − Σi∈ActiveAₛᵢ

If Os > 0.02, inspect that state again. Concentrated identifiable omissions activate the missing entities. OTHER generates no baseline rivalry hostility but remains visible as an error bound.

4A.25–4A.26 · Confidence and source disagreement

Every Asi receives confidence metadata. High: search, geography and survey/viewership agree. Moderate: two independent signals agree. Low: one weak or imputation-heavy source. Preserve disagreement—e.g., search favors Chiefs, ticket geography favors Vikings, survey favors Bears—as wider uncertainty rather than “cleaning” it away.

4A.27–4A.28 · Conditional vs population-penetrated attention

Primary model uses conditional sports attention Aˢᵖᵒʳᵗ. Diagnostics later calculate Aᵖᵒᵖsi = Cs Aˢᵖᵒʳᵗsi, where Cs measures sports-fandom penetration in the state population. The W rows also form interpretable “sports fingerprints” for each state.

4A.29 · No rivalry-driven editing of A

If Minnesota college hockey is lower than expected, the project may investigate whether the global measurement method missed evidence. It may not manually increase A because Minnesota–Wisconsin “should” receive hockey credit.

4A.30–4A.31 · Computational pipeline and files

Phase 4B’s 20-step build and file inventory are reproduced in the handoff and data-architecture sections below. Python begins systematic extraction, normalization, uncertainty, and row audits.

4A.32 · End-to-end illustrative calculation

Illustrative only If Minnesota assigns .32 to NFL attention and .82 of its NFL attention to the Vikings, A(MN,Vikings)=.2624. If Vikings rivalry R toward Green Bay is .50 and Packers state embodiment of Wisconsin is ≈1, that channel contributes about .1312 to MN→WI hostility. None of those example values are observed project results.

11 · Guardrails

Audit and stress-test plan

Sanity checks that a credible model should pass

  • Michigan–Ohio and Minnesota–Wisconsin should emerge as strong channels without being hard-coded.
  • Random state pairs with no shared sporting structure should remain near zero.
  • Shared team fandom across a border should dampen, not inflate, hatred.
  • Strongly one-sided rivalries should lose ground under reciprocal aggregation.
  • Internal rivalries should consume attention even though self-state hostility is ignored in the final contest.
  • New leagues should not leapfrog durable rivalry systems merely from one playoff series.

Structural removal tests

Strongest-edge removal

Remove a pair’s single largest entity-to-entity channel. Test whether the pair still reflects a rivalry system or collapses to one matchup.

Leave-one-sport-out

Recompute without football, baseball, basketball, hockey, soccer, college sports, and professional sports in turn.

Window and source variants

Compare five- vs ten-year windows, persistent vs current signals, alternate survey/map sources, and recency assumptions.

Uncertainty and alternative specifications

  • Monte Carlo sample plausible A, R and E distributions rather than treating point estimates as exact.
  • Report each pair’s win probability, top-three probability, rank distribution, and score interval.
  • Compare geometric-mean reciprocity with harmonic-mean and minimum variants.
  • Compare salience-only S with emotionally adjusted S⊙T.
  • Vary E definitions for ambiguous metros, national brands, and multi-state identity.
  • Preserve source disagreement as uncertainty width.
  • Require a winner that remains credible across plausible weights, not one dependent on β=.63 instead of .58.

Independent top-down validation

A separate model will work directly at the state level using sports-related negative mentions, search behavior about another state’s teams, schadenfreude when those teams lose, explicit state-versus-state language, and direct surveys asking which sports states are hated. It will initially ignore individual team-rivalry structure. The top-down and bottom-up results are compared only after both are complete; convergence strengthens confidence, disagreement becomes a research finding.
